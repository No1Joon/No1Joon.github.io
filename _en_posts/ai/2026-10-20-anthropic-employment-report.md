On October 16, 2026, Anthropic released a report analyzing employment changes at its own company

The share of new grads among new technical hires fell <mark>from 27% to 9%</mark>!

![Graph of the falling share of new-grad technical hires at Anthropic](/assets/images/ai/anthropic-employment-report/01-hero.webp)
*Graph of the falling share of new-grad technical hires at Anthropic — Source: Anthropic*

## Report overview

![Anthropic logo](/assets/images/ai/anthropic-employment-report/02-logo-anthropic.webp)
*Anthropic logo*

The title is *How employment is changing at Anthropic*
It is a technical report written by four economists at the Anthropic Institute

It draws on internal employment, AI usage, output, surveys, Slack, calendars, and hiring records
On top of that, it adds **interviews with 17 employees**

[Fewer new-grad hires]
The share of new grads among new technical hires fell while agent working time grew rapidly

[Higher output per person]
Engineers' code writing, sales deals, and legal contract handling all increased

[Human attention as the bottleneck]
Work reviewing and managing AI-generated output was added

[Changing hiring criteria]
Hiring came to value autonomy and judgment more than specialized knowledge

## Change in the new-grad share

Among staff in technical departments, the new-grad share fell from 21% in early 2025 to <mark>14%</mark>
Over the same period, the new-grad share of new technical hires fell from 27% to 9%

Job postings aimed at new grads dropped sharply from December 2025
In April 2026, engineering and research organizations lowered the share of junior-level hires

| Metric | Before | After |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| New grads among technical hires | 27% | <mark>9%</mark> |
| New grads on staff in technical departments | 21% | **14%** |
| Technical departments' share of employment | 61% | 56% |

In an April 2026 manager survey, a majority said they had changed the skills they assess in hiring because of AI
About a third of ML managers and about a fifth of SWE managers said <u>they are hiring fewer people because of AI</u>

The company's total headcount tripled in a year
Technical headcount grew too, but sales, finance, and recruiting headcount grew faster

## Agent working time

![Graph of the ratio of agent working time to human working time](/assets/images/ai/anthropic-employment-report/03-photo.webp)
*Graph of the ratio of agent working time to human working time — Source: Anthropic*

In March 2026, the working time of agents run by employees exceeded total human working hours for the first time
In August, agent working time per employee was <mark>6 times</mark> human working hours

For the research organization alone, it was 19 times!
Agent time is reasoning and tool-use time, and time spent waiting more than 30 minutes was excluded from the calculation

Human time was calculated as headcount times 40 hours a week
About 80% of interactive session time was agents waiting for human input

## Output per person

![Graph of the per-person output index by job family](/assets/images/ai/anthropic-employment-report/04-photo.webp)
*Graph of the per-person output index by job family — Source: Anthropic*

Commits per engineer reached <mark>5.1 times</mark> the December 2025 level
Data scientist queries and legal contract handling were 3.1 times, and sales deals 1.7 times

![Within-person productivity comparison before and after adopting coding agents](/assets/images/ai/anthropic-employment-report/05-photo.webp)
*Within-person productivity comparison before and after adopting coding agents — Source: Anthropic*

It compared **192 engineers** who used coding agents with 74 who did not
Lines of code added rose sharply after the month of adoption

It compared code written by the same person in the same month in the same folder
There was no difference in deletion or revert rates between code written by AI and code written by people

### 📝 The shape of work

![Change in the share of metawork by organization](/assets/images/ai/anthropic-employment-report/06-photo.webp)
*Change in the share of metawork by organization — Source: Anthropic*

**Metawork**, managing agent runs and reviewing results, increased
Its share of interactive sessions went from 10% in fall 2025 to <mark>22%</mark> in spring 2026

It calculated that reading all of Claude's output would take half of a non-technical employee's working hours
The median meeting time per employee rose 20% from a year earlier

The share of interview evaluations mentioning a history of using AI tools also rose
From 5% for technical and 7% for non-technical roles in 2024, they grew 3 times and 4 times respectively

## Points of contact in Korea

In Korea too, entry-level hiring opportunities in development roles are shrinking

By the Korea Labor Institute's count, the new-grad share of SW development job postings was 53.5% in 2022
In 2024 it fell to <mark>37.4%</mark>

| Metric | Before | After |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| New grads among Anthropic technical hires | 27% | <mark>9%</mark> |
| New-grad share of Korean SW job postings | 53.5% | **37.4%** |

In a Bank of Korea analysis, young people in employment declined from June 2022 to June 2026

Computer programming and system integration fell 16.6%
Publishing, which includes the game software industry, fell 27.4%

The two metrics measure different things, so they can't be compared directly
It is right to read only <u>that they point in the same direction</u>

## Caveats when reading it

These are results from a company that sells agents measuring itself
The report itself says Anthropic's employment patterns can't be generalized to the whole economy

The limitations the authors state themselves are these

- Tool adoption was not random; employees facing big work may have used them first
- More code does not mean better code or business results
- Adopters' output was already on a rising trend before adoption
- Survey questions changed in April 2026, making direct comparison with earlier results difficult

As things the company should do, it proposed employee equity ownership and internal redeployment of affected employees
Still, it admitted <mark>there is no way to directly stop the decline in new-grad hiring</mark>
