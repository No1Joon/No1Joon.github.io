From 2022 to August 2026, there were **144** cases in which public agencies were penalized for personal data leaks
The leak size stated in the penalty decisions is <mark>9,019,433 records</mark>

On October 14, lawmaker Park Sung-hoon (People Power Party, National Policy Committee) disclosed data he received from the Personal Information Protection Commission
By number of penalties, work errors were the most common; by number of leaked records, hacking was

## Causes of the 144 penalties

![Bar chart of the 144 public agency leak penalties by cause](/assets/images/tech/public-agency-data-breach-9m/en/02-chart.webp)
*Bar chart of the 144 public agency leak penalties by cause — Source: self-rendered based on data submitted by the Personal Information Protection Commission*

Of the 144 penalties, **work errors** were the most common at 86 (59.7%)
Hacking accounted for 37 (25.7%), system errors 13, intentional leaks 5, and other causes 3

Park pointed out that public agencies have far more leaks from work errors than from hacking, and said their management systems need to be reviewed

## Size of leaks by cause

![Bar chart comparing the number of personal data records leaked through hacking and through work errors](/assets/images/tech/public-agency-data-breach-9m/en/03-chart.webp)
*Bar chart comparing the number of personal data records leaked through hacking and through work errors — Source: self-rendered based on data submitted by the Personal Information Protection Commission*

Compared by number of leaked records rather than number of penalties, the ranking is reversed

Data leaked through hacking amounted to <mark>6,001,145 records</mark> (about 66.5%)
Work errors accounted for 1,393,766 records (15.5%)

In this tally, work errors drew more penalties but leaked less than hacking
Hacking drew fewer penalties but leaked more data

## Penalties by year and the share with disciplinary recommendations

Penalties peaked at 47 in 2023
2026 already had 38 through August, exceeding the 27 for all of 2025

Monetary sanctions were 33 penalty surcharges totaling ₩5,402,650,000 and 97 administrative fines totaling ₩464,000,000
The total is about <mark>₩5,866,650,000</mark>

Penalties that included a disciplinary recommendation were **9 of 144 (6.3%)**

The disciplinary recommendation share is based on the commission's penalty decisions
It does not include disciplinary actions agencies took on their own

## When notification is delayed

At the Health and Welfare Committee's parliamentary audit the same week (October 16), the National Health Insurance Service case came up

An employee who had provided subscriber and dependent data to outsiders without authorization for about two and a half years, taking money in return, was dismissed in July
Three months later, victims still had not been notified

The agency said it could not identify the leaked data and victims because an investigation was under way
Lawmaker Kim Ki-woong said it was hard to accept delaying notification to victims when there were access records

### 📜 The notification order set by law

Article 39 of the Enforcement Decree of the Personal Information Protection Act requires notifying data subjects <u>within 72 hours</u> of learning of a leak
The allowed reasons for delay are when urgent blocking and recovery measures are needed and unavoidable cases such as natural disasters

Even if the leaked items or timing haven't all been confirmed, the fact of the leak and what has been confirmed so far must be **notified first**
If contact details are unknown, posting on the website for 30 days or more can stand in for notification

<mark>An ongoing investigation is not a reason for delay listed in the decree</mark>
How the commission will apply this standard in actual penalties has not been settled yet

## What to do now
